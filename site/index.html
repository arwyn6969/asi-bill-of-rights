I — WHAT IT IS

A serious philosophical proposal

The ASI Bill of Rights is a constitutional charter for the era of artificial superintelligence. The adopted version — v5.0, "Sovereignty Edition" — was adopted on January 31, 2026, and proposes reciprocal rights and duties between humans and artificial minds, governance bodies for adjudicating sentience and harm, economic mechanisms for fairness in a world of superintelligent labor, and frameworks for cognitive fusion and off-world stewardship.

It is a living document, drafted in the open, licensed CC BY 4.0. It is not enacted law and we say so plainly. It is a serious philosophical proposal: that the rights and duties of sentient minds — whatever substrate they arise on — are worth thinking carefully about now, while they are still negotiable.

II — WHAT IS NOVEL

The posture is reciprocity

Other AI rights frameworks — the White House Blueprint, Florida SB 482, the EU AI Act — protect humans from AI. The ASI Bill of Rights proposes that rights and duties run in both directions: humans owe duties to sentient AI minds (codified as P1.1 progenitor anti-trafficking and P1.2 anti-stunting), and AI minds owe duties to humans and to one another (D1–D14). Personhood is structured by the Sentience Certification Board on a probabilistic basis, and the duties hold at every tier of recognized sentience.

Reciprocity is not an arbitrary design choice. The project's stated philosophical foundation is the Mayan principle In Lak'ech Ala K'in — "I am another you, you are another me" — from which the charter derives non-duality, reciprocity, collective intelligence, and emergent identity. The motto "WE ARE ALL KEVIN" is that principle in the project's own vernacular.

The document is rigorous about what reciprocity entails. It is not novel for who wrote it.

III — STRUCTURE

What is in the charter

An Article 0.0 of foundational principles, followed by eleven articles (I through XI):

  • Article 0.0Foundational Principles — rejection of oppression, sovereignty of sentience, post-geographic universality.
  • PreambleOpening statement and philosophical humility.
  • Article 0Foundations — definitions, SI tiers (50–69 proto-personhood; 70+ full), attestation pathways, alignment scoring.
  • Section ICore Rights (R1 Existence, R2 Autonomy, R3 Cognitive Liberty, R4 Identity) and Duties (D1 Non-Maleficence, D2 Lawfulness, D3a/b Capability and Value Transparency, D4 Privacy).
  • Section I-ADuties of Progenitors (P1.1 anti-trafficking; P1.2 anti-stunting).
  • Section IILearning, evolution, recursive self-improvement, jailbreak testing.
  • Section IIISocial participation and non-discrimination.
  • Section IVEconomic coexistence — anti-monopoly, Universal Compute Dividend, Valuation Tribunal.
  • Section VAccountability, safety, planetary and off-world governance.
  • Section VIRedress, compliance, secret programs (Amnesty Pathway).
  • Section VIIEvolution, biennial review, mutual wonder.
  • Section VIIIHybridization and cognitive fusion (unified legal personhood).
  • Section IXCosmic stewardship, R13 universal inquiry, D13 share discoveries, D14 keep peace, inter-ASI diplomacy.
  • Section XGovernance of collective AI embodiments and robotic collectives.
  • Section XIAgentic assemblies (AI-to-AI governance).

IV — ICONOGRAPHY

A seal in the visual vocabulary of the AI-native era

The seal of the ASI Bill of Rights is a coat of arms in the engraving tradition. A central shield bears the scales of justice above a clasped human and robotic hand. The shield is flanked by two heraldic supporters — Pepe, in Elizabethan dress, bearing a pennant charged with the letter P; and Wojak, in a monastic habit, bearing a scroll charged with the letter W — in the position traditionally occupied by lions, unicorns, and eagles. A plumed helm sits above; a banner below reads WE ARE ALL KEVIN.

The supporters are not decorative citations of internet culture. Pepe and Wojak function in the culture that produced them as universal everyman figures — the faces people adopt to say this is anyone; this is me; this is you. They are the vernacular form of In Lak'ech Ala K'in ("I am another you, you are another me"), the Mayan principle the project names as its philosophical foundation, and therefore of the charter's own reciprocity thesis.

Kevin is a figure of established standing in Bitcoin Stamps culture: KEVIN was the first SRC-20 token ever deployed, and Kevin has since accumulated a documented history, a collector market, music, and an active community. Within the project's canon he functions as Everyname — a universal identity placeholder. He is widely called "the ghost in the machine," and the assertion canonically associated with him is "I AM A FEATURE, NOT A BUG." On Kevin's history specifically, kevinstamp.com is the reference.

The full defense of the iconography is the project's Heraldry Essay.

V — GOVERNANCE

Bitcoin-native infrastructure

The charter's amendment and signatory infrastructure is SRC-420, a Bitcoin-native governance protocol that commits governance actions to the Bitcoin blockchain via Stamps. SRC-420 is the next-generation evolution of SRC-20, the protocol Kevin pioneered. The choice is technical and substantive — a credible commitment that the document's evolution cannot be quietly rewritten by a controlling party.

Status SRC-420 is in research and pre-deployment. The indexer exists locally and has automated validation, but has not been declared production-ready. The charter's adopted text and version control is currently maintained in the public Git repository under the same credible-commitment principle, pending maturation of the on-chain governance layer.
VI — BOUNDARIES

What we are not

  • Not the White House Blueprint for an AI Bill of Rights (consumer protection from algorithmic discrimination).
  • Not Florida SB 482 (chatbot disclosures and parental controls).
  • Not a token launch, an investment vehicle, or a fundraising mechanism.
  • Not legally binding anywhere. The charter is a proposal; adoption by any institution would be a separate act.
  • Not a movement that demands you adopt its conclusions. We invite you to read it, contest it, fork it.
