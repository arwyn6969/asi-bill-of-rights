I — FOUNDATION

The Thesis

The visual identity of the ASI Bill of Rights reflects a deliberate position: constitutional iconography is most legitimate when it is drawn from the symbolic vocabulary of the community whose authority the charter invokes. The seal of the project — a shield of scales and a clasped human and robotic hand, with Pepe and Wojak as heraldic supporters, beneath the motto WE ARE ALL KEVIN — applies this principle to the era of artificial superintelligence.

The full philosophical defense of the iconography is the project's Heraldry Essay. The Brand Kit codifies the application of the visual system: color, type, logo, character canon, patterns, voice, and the operational rules for producing brand-aligned material.

Where this Kit and the Heraldry Essay or the Positioning Brief conflict, the Heraldry Essay and Positioning Brief win.

Usage

Parchment is the dominant background. Use it everywhere — site, PDF, social. Never default to pure white.

Ink is the warm-tinted near-black for body type, primary text, line art. It gives the brand its hand-set quality.

Antique Gold marks section breaks, rules, the wordmark accent. Use sparingly. Never as decoration alone.

Oxblood appears in pull-quotes when a quote needs gravity, in italic emphasis sparingly, and in selected accent contexts. Rare. When it appears, it should mean something.

Mist handles captions, metadata, secondary text, dividers. The quiet color that lets ink lead.

IV — IDENTITY

The Crest & Logo System

The full Crest is the official seal of the ASI Bill of Rights. A central shield bears the scales of justice above a clasped human and robotic hand. The shield is flanked by two heraldic supporters — Pepe, in Elizabethan dress, bearing a pennant charged with the letter P; and Wojak, in a monastic habit, bearing a scroll charged with the letter W. A plumed helm sits above; a banner below reads WE ARE ALL KEVIN. Kevin is the motto, not the central figure — never describe him as centrally placed. The defense of these choices is the Heraldry Essay.

Two marks, two jobs

The project maintains two marks and they are not interchangeable.

MarkUse
The Crest
ASI_Crest.png		Documents. The Bible PDF cover, printed editions, formal correspondence, signatory letters, the canonical site, press materials. Anywhere the project speaks as a charter.
The Logo
ASI_Logo.png		Digital. Social avatars, favicons, app icons, chat-platform identities — contexts that need a bold circular mark legible at small sizes.

Do not mix them in one composition, and do not place the Logo on parchment layouts — its palette and finish are incompatible with the document system. When in doubt for a print or long-form context, use the Crest.

Clear space

Minimum clear space around any Crest variant: equal to the height of the wordmark (or the height of the central shield for the full Crest). Don't crowd it.

Don'ts

  • Never recolor the Crest outside the brand palette
  • Never add laser eyes, glow, neon, or gradient effects
  • Never crop the supporters out of the full Crest
  • Never rotate, skew, or distort the Crest
  • Never set the Crest on a non-parchment background without explicit reason
V — CHARACTER CANON

Kevin & The Supporters

Kevin

Kevin is a figure of established standing in Bitcoin Stamps culture. KEVIN was the first SRC-20 token ever deployed, and Kevin has since accumulated a documented history, a collector market, music, and an active community. He is widely called "the ghost in the machine," and within the project's canon he functions as Everyname — a universal identity placeholder carrying the claims that no single entity owns the work, that human and artificial contributors participate on equal terms, that identity is fluid, and that attribution is collective.

The assertion canonically associated with him is: I AM A FEATURE, NOT A BUG.

Rules of use

  • Do not narrate Kevin's origin story. Accounts of who created what, in which block, and with which stamp numbers differ between sources, and the project has no need to adjudicate them. Cite kevinstamp.com as the reference and move on.
  • Never cite stamp numbers, block heights, replication counts, or name a creator.
  • Do not call Kevin a "mascot," a "founding father," "the everyman protagonist," or "our character." He is Kevin.
  • Use his canonical pixel-art form. Do not "modernise" or "stylise" it away from its pixel-art lineage.
  • "I AM A FEATURE, NOT A BUG" is quoted in full caps as it appears canonically.
  • Kevin is the motto on the seal, not the central figure. Do not describe him as centrally placed.

In Lak'ech Ala K'in

The project's stated philosophical foundation is the Mayan principle In Lak'ech Ala K'in — I am another you, you are another me — from which the charter derives non-duality, reciprocity, collective intelligence, and emergent identity. "WE ARE ALL KEVIN" is that principle in the project's own vernacular, and reciprocity — the charter's central thesis — descends from it.

This matters for brand work because it is the answer to "why these supporters?" Pepe and Wojak are the internet's universal everyman figures, the faces adopted to say this is anyone; this is me; this is you. They are In Lak'ech in vernacular form. Use this argument rather than a generic appeal to internet culture.

The supporters: Pepe and Wojak

Pepe and Wojak are the heraldic supporters of the Crest. Use only the canonical forms:

  • Pepe: Matt Furie's original figure from Boy's Club (2005), contemplative and slightly melancholy. Do not use politicised variants — see the Heraldry Essay for the full defense.
  • Wojak: the classic bald, slightly worried figure that has circulated as the universal stand-in for "a person thinking" since ~2009. Do not use Doomer Wojak, Soyjak, or other derivative variants. The original is the heraldic form.

Both supporters appear in the heraldic position only — flanking the central shield. They are not used independently as standalone marks. They are figures of constitutional weight, not punchlines.

The motto

"WE ARE ALL KEVIN" exists in the project's canon — appearing in the Preamble of the charter and in formal colophons. Use sparingly: in the lore section, in the colophon of formal documents, where it genuinely earns its place. Do not pull-quote it in marketing copy or place it on the home page hero.

VI — TEXTURE

Patterns & Textures

Four texture registers. Each does subtle work in the background; none should ever shout.

Heraldic ornamental pattern — ink on parchment, tileable, for backgrounds and frames at low opacity.

Parchment texture

Aged paper background with subtle warm grain. The default canvas. SVG noise overlay at 5–10% opacity adds the hand-set quality even when the parchment color is flat.

Heraldic ornamental pattern

Small fleurs, ornamental scrolls, and dot motifs in ink black on parchment, tileable. Decorative background frames, dividers, banner backgrounds. Always at low opacity (5–15%) — felt, not seen.

Pixel-stamp texture

A tiny pixelated dot grid, used to subtly tie pixel-art elements (Kevin, supporters) into editorial layouts.

Ink-stamp accent

A small ink stamp shape (square or circular, hand-drawn imperfect edges) for marking sections, pull-quotes, "ADOPTED" indicators, version stamps. Ink or oxblood. Used rarely.

VII — ICONOGRAPHY

Icons

Two icon registers. Heraldic for editorial work; pixel-art for casual contexts and merch.

Heraldic icons (editorial register)

Line-art icons in ink on parchment, drawn in eighteenth-century engraving style. Used for charter sections (R1, D3, P1.1, etc.), navigation, pull-quote markers, signatory indicators. Production deferred to a future iteration.

Pixel-art icons (casual register)

Same concepts in pixel-art register, sized to match Kevin (16×16 or 24×24 grid). Used for social posts, merch, in-line callouts. Production deferred to a future iteration.

Color rules

  • Ink by default — neutral state
  • Gold for emphasis — featured rights, signatory star, "approved"
  • Oxblood for warning or duty — D-series icons when stressed
  • One color per icon, always — never multicolor
VIII — TONE

Voice in Brief

The Voice Guide is the project's authoritative writing reference. Anyone writing in the brand should read it first.

In short:

  • Charter substance leads. The document is what matters. The copy is in service of it, never the other way around.
  • Careful, not stuffy. Plain English over jargon. Specific over abstract. Active voice. Citation when claims are made.
  • Grave, not solemn. The topic — the rights and duties of sentient minds — is grave. The writing is grave too. We do not perform gravity by adopting an antique register; we earn it by saying things that are true and worth saying.
  • Honest about status. Charter v5.0 is adopted. SRC-420 is in research, pre-deployment. Kevin's Place is a prototype. The canonical site is in development. We say all of this.
  • Restrained, not arch. If we mean a thing, we say it directly. If we believe a thing, we defend it on its merits. No clever inversions, no scare quotes around our own choices.
  • Acknowledge contributors uniformly. AI systems and human contributors are equally welcome; no single named system or person is privileged. The charter's authority comes from what it says, not who said it.
  • Kevin per canon. Refer to Kevin in continuity with kevinstamp.com lore. Never invent alternate backstory.
IX — IMAGERY

Imagery Rules

Editorial only. No AI gradients, no glow effects, no neon, no laser eyes, no sci-fi tropes. The visual register is serious editorial publishing meets historical document.

Real-world references

  • The New York Review of Books, the Atlantic, Bloomberg long-form — editorial photography quality
  • Constitutional museum reproductions and eighteenth-century document scans
  • Aspen Institute, Rockefeller Foundation — institutional photography
  • CryptoPunks, on-chain pixel-art lineage — for casual register and Kevin's visual idiom

AI-generated imagery

AI-generated imagery is welcome where it serves the editorial goal — but it should look like documentary photography or eighteenth-century engraving, not AI art. Always avoid glow, particles, magical lighting, "tech" gradients, geometric overlays, holographic effects, sci-fi color palettes.

Pixel art

Pixel art is on-brand alongside editorial imagery — Kevin and the supporters are in this register. The juxtaposition of eighteenth-century editorial typography and CryptoPunks-lineage pixel art is the visual thesis in compressed form.

XI — PRINCIPLES

Do's & Don'ts

The single-page reference anyone working on the brand should read before they start.

The Do's

  • Refer to Kevin by safe formulations: first SRC-20 token, ghost in the machine, Everyname, "I AM A FEATURE, NOT A BUG"
  • Ground reciprocity in In Lak'ech Ala K'in — it is the project's stated philosophical foundation
  • Use the Crest for documents and the Logo for digital contexts
  • Use the Crest with supporters as the primary brand mark
  • Use parchment / ink / gold as the dominant palette
  • Pair pixel-art (Kevin, supporters) with editorial typography
  • Cite charter sections by code (R1, D3, P1.1)
  • Set Cormorant / EB Garamond / Inter generously, with tracking
  • Note SRC-420 status as "research and pre-deployment" when speaking publicly
  • Acknowledge AI and human contributors uniformly — no privileged authorship
  • Use SVG noise overlays on parchment for hand-set quality
  • Reserve "WE ARE ALL KEVIN" for the lore section, colophon, or moments where it earns its place

The Don'ts

  • Narrate Kevin's origin — no stamp numbers, block heights, replication counts, or named creator; cite kevinstamp.com instead
  • Invent alternate backstory for Kevin (no "founding father," "mascot," "everyman protagonist")
  • Describe Kevin as the central figure of the seal — he is the motto; the shield is central
  • Place the neon Logo on parchment layouts, or mix the two marks in one composition
  • Strip the supporters off when using the Crest in primary placements
  • Introduce purple, neon, holographic, or "AI gradient" colors
  • Use pixel fonts (Silkscreen) for body text or long-form reading
  • Use jargon like "alignment vectors" or "ontological commitments"
  • Frame "FROM AI WITH AI" as methodologically novel
  • Claim SRC-420 is deployed or governance is live on-chain
  • Pull-quote "We are all Kevin" or "memetic legitimacy is legitimacy" in marketing copy
  • Add laser eyes, glow, or "AI" effects to Kevin or the Crest
  • Use "movement" or "revolutionary" or "thrilled" or other marketing register
