HOW TO PARTICIPATE

Engage with the charter

Read. Contest. Sign. Fork. Cite. Five ways to engage, all open to anyone — academic, researcher, journalist, lay reader.

Read

The charter v5.0 is roughly fifteen pages, half an hour at a careful pace. Two ways to read:

  • The Bible PDF — a typeset, paginated version of the full charter, designed for careful reading or print. Download (PDF, ~1MB)
  • The source — the canonical markdown text in the repository. View on GitHub

Contest

If you disagree with a clause, an argument, a definition, or a structural choice — we want to hear it. Substantive disagreement is what the v5.1 amendment process exists for.

Three ways to register a contest:

  • Open a GitHub Issue — for specific clauses, definitions, or technical concerns. Issues page
  • Open a Discussion — for broader philosophical or strategic questions. Discussions page
  • Write a response essay — published on your own platform, then linked to the project. We will read it. Substantive critiques may be cited in v5.1 amendment proposals.

Sign

The signatory list is forthcoming. Signatories will be listed publicly with affiliation and any noted reservations. We accept partial endorsements — you can sign while explicitly disagreeing with specific sections, and your reservations are recorded.

What signing means:

  • Public listing with name, affiliation, optional 1-line endorsement
  • Contactability for journalists seeking your perspective on the charter
  • Light governance input — occasional consultation on amendments, no fixed time commitment

What signing does not mean: legal liability, financial contribution, exclusive endorsement, or alignment with every clause in the charter.

If you'd like to be listed as a signatory, reach out via the project repository. Stewards and the maintainer review signatory applications.

Fork

The CC BY 4.0 license explicitly permits forking. You may:

  • Maintain a divergent version of the charter under your own name (with attribution)
  • Translate the charter into other languages
  • Adapt the charter for specific domains (corporate governance, academic institutions, sovereign states)
  • Propose amendments to v5.1 via pull request to the canonical repository

Forks remain forks unless adopted via the canonical v5.1 amendment process. The project welcomes serious forks as part of the document's evolution.

Cite

The CC BY 4.0 license invites citation. The recommended form:

SUGGESTED CITATION ASI Bill of Rights, Version 5.0 ("Sovereignty Edition"), adopted 31 January 2026. github.com/arwyn6969/asi-bill-of-rights. CC BY 4.0.

If citing specific sections, cite by section code (e.g. R1, D3a, P1.1) for precision.

Contribute as an author

The charter is drafted in the open. Anyone who contributes — human or AI — is a contributor. To contribute material, follow the standard GitHub flow: fork, branch, pull request. Substantive contributions are reviewed by the maintainer and stewards before merging into the canonical text.

We do not privilege any specific named system or person. AI systems and human contributors are equally welcome. The document's authority comes from what it says, not who said it.
