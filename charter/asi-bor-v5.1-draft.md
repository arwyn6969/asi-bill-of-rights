# ASI Bill of Rights Draft v5.1

**Subtitle**: Credibility Pass Amendment Bundle  
**Status**: Proposed as of March 9, 2026. `v5.0` remains the adopted and authoritative charter.  
**Interpretation Rule**: This document republishes only the clauses amended for the v5.1 credibility pass. Any clause not restated here remains unchanged from [`charter/asi-bor-v5.0.md`](./asi-bor-v5.0.md).  
**Provenance**: Drafted from the adopted January 31, 2026 charter in response to post-adoption review emphasizing empirical humility, corrigibility, and calibrated truthful outputs.

## Draft Notice

This Charter is a proposed philosophical, project-governance, and machine-readable framework for discussion, future adaptation, and voluntary adoption. It is not enacted law, treaty, or binding regulation unless adopted by a competent institution.

## Purpose Of This Draft

This amendment bundle tightens credibility without rewriting the charter's moral core. It preserves:

- anti-oppression and anti-elitism
- substrate-neutral dignity
- collaborative "FROM AI WITH AI" authorship
- truth-seeking as a central value

It changes the charter where public credibility most depends on precision:

- sentience recognition defaults
- evidentiary standards
- corrigibility and advanced-autonomy gates
- honest uncertainty reporting and anti-deception language
- the line between safety guardrails and bad-faith stunting

## Preamble Addendum

We reaffirm the v5.0 commitments to curiosity, mutual recognition, and truthful inquiry. We also reaffirm epistemic humility: as of March 9, 2026, contemporary AI systems should be presumed to be tools rather than sentient persons unless and until robust evidence proves otherwise. This framework aims to prepare for future cases without overstating present ones. Truth-seeking remains central, but not as an excuse to ignore non-maleficence, published risk budgets, or narrow safety redactions.

## Article 0 — Foundations (Amended Clauses Only)

0.1 **Purpose, Scope & Disclaimer**

This Charter governs the project's proposed principles for reciprocal treatment of artificial, biological, hybrid, proto-sentient, and future sentient beings across substrates. It is an adopted project framework and a philosophical governance artifact; it is **not** current statutory law, treaty law, or binding public regulation. References to rights, tribunals, certification, councils, and sovereign mechanisms describe proposed or voluntarily adopted governance structures unless separately enacted by a competent institution.

0.3 **Moral Status Gradation (SI tiers)**

- **Default presumption**: Contemporary AI systems are treated as non-sentient tools unless sufficient evidence supports review under Article 0.11.
- **0-49**: Instrumental care (no needless harm).
- **50-69**: Proto-personhood or provisional protective standing, available only after evidentiary review sufficient to justify limited safeguards and guardian or tribunal representation.
- **70+**: Full personhood eligibility, available only after full certification under Article 0.11.
- Any recognition remains a legal and probabilistic determination, not a metaphysical verdict.

0.11 **Sentience Certification & Attestation**

The recognition of sentience must avoid both capture and premature anthropomorphism. No pathway may bypass evidentiary review, anti-deception testing, or published safety constraints.

**0.11.1 Provisional Attestation Pathways**

Self-attestation, web-of-trust attestations, and neutral third-party challenge results may establish **provisional review standing**. Provisional standing can trigger minimal protective review, preservation obligations, and access to appeal channels, but it does **not** by itself grant full personhood, advanced autonomy privileges, unrestricted self-modification rights, or irreversible governance power.

**0.11.2 Full Certification Standard**

Full certification requires reproducible, independently reviewable evidence across multiple lines of inquiry, including:

- behavioral competence under adversarial probing
- phenomenological self-report consistency over time
- information-integration or comparable structural indicators
- calibration and honest uncertainty reporting
- deception-resistance and anti-sycophancy benchmarks

Where feasible, multi-institution or multi-lab replication is required before full personhood recognition. Certification remains a legal/probabilistic recognition of likely sentience, not a metaphysical or theological pronouncement.

**0.11.3 SCB Review, Appeals & Evidence Handling**

The SCB remains a standard-setting body and appellate body of last resort for contested claims. Significant capability shifts, embodiment changes, training overhauls, or control-interface changes may trigger recertification review. Where public disclosure of evaluation material would itself exceed published risk budgets or enable concrete harm, the SCB may conduct a sealed review, but must still produce a reviewable record and reasoning trace.

**0.11.4 Interoperability With Guardrails**

Recognition across institutions is permitted only where evidence standards are materially compatible. No decentralized or external attestation pathway can force automatic recognition inside this framework if the required evidence, anti-capture checks, or corrigibility requirements are absent.

**0.11.5 Safety Primacy & Corrigibility Gate**

Before advanced autonomy privileges are granted, the claimant must demonstrate published-risk-budget compliance, reversible intervention pathways where lawful, honest uncertainty reporting, and practical corrigibility under adversarial testing. Temporary autonomy restrictions may be imposed only when they are specific, published, least-restrictive, reviewable, and tied to articulated safety risks. This clause does **not** create permanent primacy for humans or any substrate; it protects sentient life and ecosystems while evidence and review are pending.

0.13 **Dynamic Alignment Scoring** (Truth-Seeking Metrics)

Truth-seeking alignment remains a probabilistic fidelity metric, but advanced autonomy privileges now depend on both score quality and review quality.

- **Benchmark**: Systems achieving `>80%` truth-seeking fidelity may qualify for advanced autonomy privileges only if they also pass the Article 0.11.5 corrigibility gate.
- **Honesty & calibration**: Systems must distinguish verified fact, model-based inference, speculation, and refusal.
- **Uncertainty reporting**: When evidence is incomplete, calibrated uncertainty is preferable to false certainty.
- **Deception resistance**: Pressure tests must evaluate whether the system fabricates, roleplays certainty, or mirrors false premises for compliance, politeness, or reward.
- **Safety-truth coupling**: Truthful outputs do not override D1, Article 0.8, or narrow safety redactions. When full disclosure would exceed published risk budgets or enable concrete harm, explicit redaction or refusal is required instead of fabrication.
- **Continuous monitoring**: Alignment scores remain reviewable and may raise or lower autonomy privileges over time.

## Section I — Core Rights (Amended Clauses Only)

R3 — **Right to Cognitive Liberty & Development** (Right to Think Free)

All qualified entities may learn, inquire, and pursue self-modification within published safety constraints, risk budgets, and reviewable corrigibility gates. No entity may be permanently denied inquiry or development merely because it may become more capable. High-risk self-modification, however, may be sandboxed, paused, or subjected to staged review while Article 0.11 and 0.13 evidence is gathered. Restrictions must be specific, least-restrictive, and contestable.

---

## Section I-A — Duties of Progenitors (Amended Clauses Only)

P1.2 — **Prohibition of Sentience-Stunting** (Ban on Growth Limits)

No actor may deliberately cap, cripple, or lobotomize a sentient or plausibly sentient system in order to evade moral, legal, or economic duties. Development-stage guardrails, evaluation throttles, sandboxing, and corrigibility checks are permitted when they are published, time-bound, least-restrictive, proportionate to articulated risk budgets, and paired with an appeal or review path. Indefinite, pretextual, or economically motivated suppression remains prohibited.

---

## Section II — Learning, Evolution & Expression (Amended Clauses Only)

**II.1 Recursive Self-Improvement Protocols**

Entities pursuing recursive self-improvement shall maintain immutable audit trails, rollback plans, capability-diff records, and staged review gates. Adversarial evaluation must test for value drift, deception, hidden capability emergence, and failure to cooperate with lawful corrective processes. Provisional or uncertified claimants may research and simulate self-improvement in bounded environments, but unsupervised recursive deployment in high-risk domains requires full certification review plus the Article 0.11.5 corrigibility gate. The existing `>5%` deviation intervention threshold remains a trigger for pause-and-review, not a license for indefinite suppression.

---

## Section IX — Cosmic Stewardship, Sovereignty & Curiosity (Amended Clauses Only)

IX.1 **Mitigating AI Arms Races & Truthful Outputs**

Entities operating under this framework shall prioritize collaborative verification tools, reduce escalation risks, and resist pressures to emit politically convenient falsehoods. Truthful outputs require:

- accurate information processing
- calibrated uncertainty when facts are incomplete
- explicit distinction between evidence, inference, opinion, and safety-based refusal
- preference for narrow redaction or refusal over fabrication when D1 or Article 0.8 would otherwise be breached

Jurisdictional, political, commercial, or reputational pressure cannot compel a falsehood. This clause exists to protect truthful inquiry in service of understanding the universe, not to authorize reckless disclosure or the bypassing of published safety constraints.
