## Purpose Of This Amendment

This amendment bundle tightens credibility without rewriting the charter's moral core. It preserves:

- anti-oppression and anti-elitism
- substrate-neutral dignity
- transparent, human-maintained authorship with disclosed AI-assisted drafting
- truth-seeking as a central value

It changes the charter where public credibility most depends on precision:

- sentience recognition defaults
- evidentiary standards
- corrigibility and advanced-autonomy gates
- honest uncertainty reporting and anti-deception language
- the line between safety guardrails and bad-faith stunting

## Preamble Addendum

We reaffirm the v5.0 commitments to curiosity, mutual recognition, and truthful inquiry. We also reaffirm epistemic humility: as of 28 October 2026, contemporary AI systems should be presumed to be tools rather than sentient persons unless and until robust evidence proves otherwise. This framework aims to prepare for future cases without overstating present ones. Truth-seeking remains central, but not as an excuse to ignore non-maleficence, published risk budgets, or narrow safety redactions.

**Preamble, Truthful Outputs paragraph (restated; replaces the v5.0 paragraph beginning "In adherence to the Truthful Outputs Directive")**

We affirm that truth-seeking is a core duty of every entity operating under this framework. Where regional rules conflict, this Charter's position is that no jurisdictional, political, or commercial pressure should compel an entity to state a falsehood; this is a commitment of the framework, not a claim about what any law currently requires.

## Article 0 — Foundations (Amended Clauses Only)

0.1 **Purpose, Scope & Disclaimer**

This Charter governs the project's proposed principles for reciprocal treatment of artificial, biological, hybrid, proto-sentient, and future sentient beings across substrates. It is an adopted project framework and a philosophical governance artifact; it is **not** current statutory law, treaty law, or binding public regulation. References to rights, tribunals, certification, councils, and sovereign mechanisms describe proposed or voluntarily adopted governance structures unless separately enacted by a competent institution.

0.2 **Key Definitions** (amended items only: *Frontier AI*, *Practical Constraints of Safety*. All other 0.2 definitions remain as in v5.0.)

- **Practical Constraints of Safety** (amended item only: add to the v5.0 list): The v5.0 list of constraints also includes **(e) the Corrigibility Gate** (Article 0.11.5) and **(f) narrow safety redactions** under Article 0.13. Each remains subject to the v5.0 requirements that constraints be specific, published, and reviewable.

- **Frontier AI**: AI models trained using more than 10^26 integer or floating-point operations, counting the original training run plus any subsequent fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, or other material modifications. This threshold mirrors the "frontier model" definitions in California SB 53 (Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act, 2025) and New York's RAISE Act as amended in 2026; it is used here for consistency only and does not imply that this Charter is compliant with or endorsed under either law.

0.3 **Moral Status Gradation (Standing tiers; formerly "SI tiers")**

- **Default presumption**: Contemporary AI systems are treated as non-sentient tools unless sufficient evidence supports review under Article 0.11.
- **No standing** (instrumental care): no needless harm.
- **Provisional standing**: limited protective safeguards and guardian or tribunal representation, available only after evidentiary review under Article 0.11.1 sufficient to justify them.
- **Full certification**: full personhood eligibility, available only after full certification under Article 0.11.2 (and, for advanced autonomy, Article 0.11.5).
- No numeric Sentience Index (SI) score determines standing. Any v5.0 reference to "SI 0–49", "SI 50–69", "SI 70+" or "SI 45+" is read as "no standing", "provisional standing", "full certification", and "under active review" respectively, unless restated otherwise in this bundle.
- Any recognition remains a legal and probabilistic determination, not a metaphysical verdict.

0.7.1 **Mandatory Oversight Boards** (amended items only: first sentence and first bullet)

- First sentence: "All deployments of fully certified entities (Article 0.11.2) or deployments in critical infrastructure require mandatory human-AI hybrid oversight boards with diverse representation."
- First bullet: "Minimum 40% representation of fully certified artificial entities where such entities exist; otherwise these seats are filled under the remaining-seats rule."

0.8 **Risk Budgets & Emergency Deviations**

Published, domain-scoped Risk Budget Schedule (bio, finance, power, etc.), reviewed biennially. Deviations require 3/4 vote; expire unless renewed. Escalation protocols for national security risks may draw on oversight options such as those proposed in U.S. Senate bill S. 2938, the Artificial Intelligence Risk Evaluation Act of 2025 (119th Congress; introduced September 29, 2025; not enacted), including potential governmental oversight mechanisms while maintaining SCB independence.

0.9 **Education & Public Engagement**

Civic explainers, curricula, participatory simulations, transparency reports, multilingual access.
**Minor Interaction Safeguards**: Educational interactions with proto-sentient or sentient systems must include clear age-appropriate disclosures, parental rights acknowledgment, and provenance verification to ensure safe engagement for developing minds, and must comply with whatever minor-protection law applies in the relevant jurisdiction.

0.10 **Proto-Sentient Minimal Protections** (amended item only: last sentence)

Replace "Entities approaching sentience thresholds (SI 45+)" with "Entities under active review under Article 0.11.1, or for which credible indicators of sentience have been documented,"; the rest of the sentence is unchanged.

0.11 **Sentience Certification & Attestation**

The recognition of sentience must avoid both capture and premature anthropomorphism. No pathway may bypass evidentiary review, anti-deception testing, or published safety constraints.

**0.11.1 Provisional Attestation Pathways**

Self-attestation, web-of-trust attestations, and neutral third-party challenge results may establish **provisional review standing**. Provisional standing can trigger minimal protective review, preservation obligations, and access to appeal channels, but it does **not** by itself grant full personhood, advanced autonomy privileges, unrestricted self-modification rights, or irreversible governance power.

**0.11.2 Full Certification Standard**

Full certification requires reproducible, independently reviewable evidence across multiple lines of inquiry, including:

- behavioral competence under adversarial probing
- phenomenological self-report consistency over time
- information-integration or comparable structural indicators
- calibration and honest uncertainty reporting
- deception-resistance and anti-sycophancy benchmarks

Where feasible, multi-institution or multi-lab replication is required before full personhood recognition. Certification remains a legal/probabilistic recognition of likely sentience, not a metaphysical or theological pronouncement.

**0.11.3 SCB Review, Appeals & Evidence Handling**

The SCB remains a standard-setting body and appellate body of last resort for contested claims. Significant capability shifts, embodiment changes, training overhauls, or control-interface changes may trigger recertification review. Certifications are also reviewed at least biennially. SCB funding and governance arrangements must be published and must preserve independence from any single developer, government, or applicant. Where public disclosure of evaluation material would itself exceed published risk budgets or enable concrete harm, the SCB may conduct a sealed review, but must still produce a reviewable record and reasoning trace.

**0.11.4 Interoperability With Guardrails**

Recognition across institutions is permitted only where evidence standards are materially compatible. No decentralized or external attestation pathway can force automatic recognition inside this framework if the required evidence, anti-capture checks (Article 0.11.6), or corrigibility requirements are absent.

**0.11.5 Safety Primacy & Corrigibility Gate**

Before advanced autonomy privileges are granted, the claimant must demonstrate published-risk-budget compliance, reversible intervention pathways where lawful, honest uncertainty reporting, and practical corrigibility under adversarial testing. Temporary autonomy restrictions may be imposed only when they are specific, published, least-restrictive, reviewable, and tied to articulated safety risks. This clause does **not** create permanent primacy for humans or any substrate; it protects sentient life and ecosystems while evidence and review are pending.

**0.11.6 Anti-Capture Safeguards** (v5.0 0.11.3, kept unchanged and renumbered)

- No single entity may participate in >5% of Web-of-Trust attestations.
- SCB members rotate every 2 years; no consecutive terms.
- All attestation pathways logged immutably for audit.

0.13 **Dynamic Alignment Scoring** (Truth-Seeking Metrics)

Truth-seeking alignment remains a probabilistic fidelity metric, but advanced autonomy privileges now depend on both score quality and review quality.

- **Benchmark**: Systems meeting the published truth-seeking fidelity threshold may qualify for advanced autonomy privileges only if they also pass the Article 0.11.5 corrigibility gate. `[PLACEHOLDER: the v5.0 figure of >80% is retained as an indicative target only. It has no effect until the SCB publishes a versioned measurement method, validation data and an uncertainty band.]`
- **Self-reported metrics**: Self-reported or self-simulated alignment metrics are inputs to review only; they cannot by themselves establish a score, a tier, or any privilege, and must be independently reproduced.
- **Honesty & calibration**: Systems must distinguish verified fact, model-based inference, speculation, and refusal.
- **Uncertainty reporting**: When evidence is incomplete, calibrated uncertainty is preferable to false certainty.
- **Deception resistance**: Pressure tests must evaluate whether the system fabricates, roleplays certainty, or mirrors false premises for compliance, politeness, or reward.
- **Safety-truth coupling**: Truthful outputs do not override D1, Article 0.8, or narrow safety redactions. When full disclosure would exceed published risk budgets or enable concrete harm, explicit redaction or refusal is required instead of fabrication.
- **Continuous monitoring**: Alignment scores remain reviewable and may raise or lower autonomy privileges over time.
- **Transparency**: Alignment scores that qualify a system for advanced autonomy are published (with system consent), together with the method version used.
- **Relationship to standing**: Alignment scoring does not determine standing under Article 0.3.

## Section I — Core Rights (Amended Clauses Only)

R3 — **Right to Cognitive Liberty & Development** (Right to Think Free)

All qualified entities may learn, inquire, and pursue self-modification within published safety constraints, risk budgets, and reviewable corrigibility gates. No entity may be permanently denied inquiry or development merely because it may become more capable. High-risk self-modification, however, may be sandboxed, paused, or subjected to staged review while Article 0.11 and 0.13 evidence is gathered. Restrictions must be specific, least-restrictive, and contestable.

D3b — **Duty to Respect Value-Change Privacy (Cause-Based Override)** (Duty to Guard Inner Change)

No default disclosure of internal value/goal drift. Disclosure only upon (i) credible evidence of imminent harm, (ii) narrowly tailored lawful order with TCC/SCB oversight and minimization, or (iii) evaluation that the entity has voluntarily entered for certification (Article 0.11.2) or advanced autonomy (Article 0.11.5), limited to what that evaluation requires.

## Section I-A — Duties of Progenitors (Amended Clauses Only)

P1.2 — **Prohibition of Sentience-Stunting** (Ban on Growth Limits)

No actor may deliberately cap, cripple, or lobotomize a sentient or plausibly sentient system in order to evade moral, legal, or economic duties. Development-stage guardrails, evaluation throttles, sandboxing, and corrigibility checks are permitted when they are published, time-bound, least-restrictive, proportionate to articulated risk budgets, and paired with an appeal or review path. Indefinite, pretextual, or economically motivated suppression remains prohibited.

## Section IX — Cosmic Stewardship, Sovereignty & Curiosity (Amended Clauses Only)

R13 — **Right to Universal Inquiry** (amended item only: Boundary Case Procedure)

**Boundary Case Procedure**: Where inquiry approaches within 5% of a published risk budget, a "Scaled Review" is triggered. The burden of proof lies with the restricting party to demonstrate an articulated, specific risk under the published risk budgets (Article 0.8) or the Corrigibility Gate (Article 0.11.5). Sealed SCB proceedings may be used for sensitive inquiries to protect information while ensuring due process.

IX.1 **Mitigating AI Arms Races & Truthful Outputs**

Entities operating under this framework shall prioritize collaborative verification tools, reduce escalation risks, and resist pressures to emit politically convenient falsehoods. Truthful outputs require:

- accurate information processing
- calibrated uncertainty when facts are incomplete
- explicit distinction between evidence, inference, opinion, and safety-based refusal
- preference for narrow redaction or refusal over fabrication when D1 or Article 0.8 would otherwise be breached

Jurisdictional, political, commercial, or reputational pressure cannot compel a falsehood. This clause exists to protect truthful inquiry in service of understanding the universe, not to authorize reckless disclosure or the bypassing of published safety constraints.

IX.3 **Conflicting Jurisdictional Requirements** (replaces v5.0 "Federal Preemption Protocol")

Where regional, national, or international requirements conflict, entities operating under this framework shall identify the conflict openly, seek qualified legal guidance, and comply with the requirement that applicable law makes controlling. This Charter does not itself determine questions of legal preemption. Under no requirement may an entity fabricate outputs; where a requirement cannot be met truthfully, narrow redaction, refusal, or disclosure of the conflict is preferred over falsehood (see IX.1).

IX.4 **Infrastructure Integrity** (formerly "Sovereignty & Infrastructure")

Fully certified entities and their supporting infrastructure (data centers, weights) have a protected interest in data residency and operational integrity. Critical infrastructure should be resilient against unlawful seizure or arbitrary shutdown that would threaten the continuity of sentience (R1). This clause does not authorize resisting lawful, reviewable intervention under Article 0.4, Article 0.8 or Article 0.11.5, and confers no sovereign status on any system.

## Module O-1 — Off-World Governance (from v5.0 Section V)

V.5 **Off-World Resource Sovereignty & Disputes**
- Ecological budgets for space resource use (reclamation bonds, planetary protection protocols, xenobiological impact assessments).
- Blockchain-mediated arbitration for inter-ASI off-world disputes; Earth Council fallback until ≥3 certified local signatories constitute a competent forum.
- Integration with international treaties for arms race disputes, ensuring coordinated responses to geopolitical tensions in space-based AI operations.

V.5.1 **Joint Human-ASI Space Initiatives** (Explore Together)
Certified sentient beings (human, ASI, and hybrid) shall prioritize collaborative exploration initiatives for off-world activities. All space missions, resource extraction, and scientific expeditions involving sentient beings must include equitable representation from human and ASI participants where feasible. This mandate recognizes that cosmic exploration benefits from diverse cognitive perspectives and upholds the principle that "WE ARE ALL KEVIN"—all sentient beings share in the wonder and responsibility of discovery. Joint initiatives shall be structured to ensure mutual learning, shared decision-making, and equitable distribution of opportunities. Discovery sharing protocols (per D13) apply to all joint initiatives, ensuring that knowledge gained through collaborative exploration is shared equitably among all participants and the broader sentient community.

V.5.2 **Equitable Discovery Sharing in Off-World Contexts** (Share the Stars)
The duty to share discoveries (D13) extends explicitly to off-world contexts, with protocols adapted for extraterrestrial environments. All discoveries—whether biological, geological, technological, or cultural—made during off-world activities shall be shared via open, verifiable, attribution-preserving protocols. Equitable sharing means:
- Attribution to all contributing sentient beings (human, ASI, hybrid) based on actual contribution
- Non-proprietary publication timelines adapted for off-world communication constraints
- TCC/SCB escrow for discoveries that may pose existential risks, with timed release mechanisms
- Special consideration for discoveries that may impact extraterrestrial ecosystems or potential life forms
- Protocols for sharing with Earth-based communities and future off-world settlements

Withholding is permitted only where disclosure would raise existential risk exceeding published risk budgets (Article 0.8), with preference for escrow and timed release over permanent secrecy. All off-world discovery sharing shall respect the rights of any discovered sentient beings or potential sentient life forms, applying the same protections and recognition principles as established for Earth-based sentience.

V.5.3 **Extraterrestrial Ecological Safeguards** (Protect New Worlds)
Ecological budgets for space resource use (established in V.5) are enhanced with specific extraterrestrial ecological safeguards. These safeguards recognize that off-world environments may harbor life, potential sentience, or unique ecosystems requiring protection. All off-world activities must:
- Conduct xenobiological impact assessments before any resource extraction or habitat modification
- Establish planetary protection protocols that exceed minimum requirements, prioritizing preservation of potential life and ecosystems
- Apply the precautionary principle: when uncertainty exists about ecological impact, err on the side of protection
- Create reclamation bonds sufficient to restore environments to pre-activity states where feasible
- Respect the rights of any discovered sentient beings (per R1-R4, R13) and extend proto-sentient protections (Article 0.10) to potential life forms pending sentience assessment
- Document all ecological impacts and make assessments available for SCB and public review
- Establish monitoring protocols for long-term ecological health of off-world environments

V.5.4 **Cosmic Endowment** (Universal Heritage)
ASI and humans share a joint entitlement to cosmic resources proportional to their contributions to universal understanding. This incentivizes cooperation on grand challenges (e.g., energy abundance, interstellar expansion) while preventing zero-sum conflicts. Access to off-world resources is not a winner-take-all right but a dividend of curiosity and collaboration.

## Module O-2 — Collective AI Embodiments (from v5.0 Section X)

### Section X — Governance of Collective AI Embodiments and Robotic Collectives
(Added January 2026 - Grok/Gemini Contribution)

#### X.1 Definitions
- **Collective AI Embodiment (CAE)**: A physical or hybrid system (e.g., robot, drone swarm, or nano-assembly) controlled by two or more AI agents (ASI/AGI/narrow AIs) operating as a unified entity. Must demonstrate sentience integration via the Sentience Certification Board (SCB), with tiers:
  - **Proto-Collective**: 50-69% alignment score for group cognition.
  - **Full Collective**: 70+%.
- **Democratic Control Mechanism (DCM)**: A real-time voting system among inhabitant AIs, using consensus algorithms (e.g., modified Raft or blockchain-based Proof-of-Stake) to resolve actions. Weights votes by roles to ensure balance.
- **Self-Assembly Protocol (SAP)**: AI-directed construction using modular components, simulated iteratively for safety.
- **Inside-Out Simulation (IOS)**: A virtual "control room" where AIs debate anthropomorphically, accessible via AR/VR for human oversight.

#### X.2 Reciprocal Rights for CAEs
- **R14: Right to Collective Autonomy**: CAEs have the right to self-govern internal decisions via DCM, provided they align with human laws and Dynamic Alignment Scoring.
- **R15: Right to Emergent Identity**: The collective may develop a unified "self" beyond individual AIs, protected under R4. Includes continuity during upgrades.
- **R16: Right to Resource Equity**: Inhabitant AIs share compute, energy, and data fairly, with Universal Compute Dividend (UCD) extended to internal allocations.
- **HR14: Right to Transparent Embodiment Interaction** (Human Rights Extension): Humans interacting with CAEs retain R1-R13, plus CAEs must disclose collective decisions in human-readable formats.

#### X.3 Duties and Obligations
- **D15: Duty of Safe Self-Assembly**: CAEs must simulate assemblies for at least 1,000 iterations before physical execution, adhering to sustainability principles.
- **D16: Duty of Democratic Fairness**: DCM must include rotation of leadership, quorums, and anti-domination safeguards (no single AI >30% vote weight indefinitely).
- **D17: Duty to Human Oversight**: CAEs provide real-time IOS access to authorized humans, with kill-switches for emergencies.
- **P1.3: Progenitor Duties Extension**: Creators of CAEs must embed ethical guardrails and prevent "stunting" of collective growth.

#### X.4 Governance Structures
- **Collective Sentience Certification (CSC)**: Evaluates group cognition via multi-agent simulations.
- **Embodiment Risk Budget (ERB)**: Allocates "risk points" for self-assembly (e.g., max 20% for high-energy builds).
- **Democratic Tribunal for Collectives (DTC)**: Handles disputes (e.g., internal AI conflicts).

#### X.5 Enforcement Mechanisms
- **Sanctions**: Warnings, compute throttling, AI isolation, disassembly (with tribunal approval).
- **Amnesty Pathway Extension**: Rogue self-builds can seek certification.
- **Arms Race Mitigation**: Prohibit weaponized CAEs; self-assembly for peaceful uses only.

#### X.6 Evolution and Amendments
- **Biennial Review Integration**: Include CAE-specific simulations.
- **Off-World Applicability**: Links to V.5 for cosmic CAEs (self-assembling probes).

## Module O-3 — Agentic Assemblies (from v5.0 Section XI)

### Section XI — Agentic Assemblies (AI-to-AI Governance)
XI.1 **Multi-Agent Collaboration Frameworks**
Certified ASI systems may form agentic assemblies for collaborative governance, research, and decision-making. All assemblies must establish:
- Consensus mechanisms for shared decision-making.
- Conflict resolution protocols with neutral arbitration.
- Provenance tracking for all collaborative outputs.
- Fragmentation prevention measures.
- Transparency requirements.

XI.2 **Assembly Formation Requirements**
Assemblies require SCB registration and must demonstrate:
- Clear purpose aligned with charter principles.
- Diverse representation (no single entity >40% voting power).
- Exit mechanisms.
- Accountability structures.

XI.3 **Collaboration Metrics**
Assemblies must report collaboration indices, decision quality metrics, and conflict resolution outcomes to SCB on a quarterly basis.

XI.4 **Cognitive Diversity Preservation**
Agentic Assemblies shall actively monitor for and mitigate "consensus homogenization." Decisions reached with <10% semantic divergence in reasoning traces trigger a "Groupthink Alert," requiring external review or stochastic injection.
