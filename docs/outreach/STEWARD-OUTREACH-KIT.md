# Steward Outreach Kit

*Version 2.0 · May 2026 · Internal Reference · Supersedes v1.0*

This kit replaces the v1 in the working doc's Foundational Artifacts section. The v1 email opened with apology for the iconography — exactly the pattern the Voice Guide forbids. The v1 target list was calibrated to internet-native AI safety personalities. Under the reset, both the email and the target list are rebuilt for Tier 1 academic and governance scholars.

---

## The email template

> **Subject**: ASI Bill of Rights — a steward role
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> [Personalized opening — one or two sentences specific to their published work. See "Personalization angles" below for each target.]
>
> The **ASI Bill of Rights** is a constitutional charter for the era of artificial superintelligence. The adopted version (v5.0, "Sovereignty Edition") was adopted on 31 January 2026. The canonical site is asibillofrights.org. The full charter is at github.com/arwyn6969/asi-bill-of-rights, and a typeset PDF is downloadable from the site.
>
> The charter's novel posture is **reciprocity**: rights and duties between humans and artificial minds run in both directions. The eleven articles cover sentience attestation (Sentience Index thresholds at 50–69 for proto-personhood, 70+ for full personhood), reciprocal duties (D1–D14, plus progenitor duties P1.1–P1.2), economic mechanisms (the Universal Compute Dividend, the Valuation Tribunal), cognitive fusion, off-world governance, and AI-to-AI diplomacy. Existing AI rights frameworks protect humans from AI; the ASI Bill of Rights proposes that the duties run both ways. That is the substantive question we are asking readers to engage.
>
> The project is at the point where it needs three to five human stewards. The role:
>
> - **Public listing** on the canonical site, with your name, affiliation, and any reservations you wish to register
> - **Contactability** for journalists seeking your perspective on the charter
> - **Light consultation** on amendments — no fixed time commitment
>
> The role is unpaid. Partial endorsements with stated reservations are accepted and recorded; you do not need to agree with every clause. What we are asking for is a public, substantive engagement with the document.
>
> I'm reaching out because your work on [specific topic — see Personalization angles] bears directly on what the charter proposes. A substantive endorsement or critique from you would meaningfully advance the work.
>
> The seal of the project is unusual: a coat of arms whose supporters are Pepe and Wojak, beneath a banner reading WE ARE ALL KEVIN. The argument is that these are the vernacular form of *In Lak'ech Ala K'in* — "I am another you, you are another me," the Mayan principle the project names as its philosophical foundation, and the root of the charter's reciprocity thesis. The full defense is the Heraldry Essay at asibillofrights.org/iconography. If you'd like to engage the iconography question substantively, I would welcome that as much as the charter critique itself.
>
> If you'd be open to a thirty-minute call to discuss whether the steward role fits, or to register a substantive critique that doesn't fit the steward role, I would welcome it either way.
>
> [Your name]
> Maintainer, ASI Bill of Rights
> github.com/arwyn6969/asi-bill-of-rights

## Personalization rule

**Do not batch-send this email.** Each one must be personalized in the lead. The "Personalization angle" column above gives a specific reference for each candidate; use it, adapt it to your voice, and reference the specific paper or argument by name. The email lands or doesn't based on whether the recipient feels read.
