## What this is

The Voice Guide is the operational reference for anyone writing on behalf of the ASI Bill of Rights — site copy, press materials, social posts, signatory letters, outreach emails, responses to critics. It is the companion to the Positioning Brief: the Brief states what the project is and means; the Voice Guide states how the project speaks.

Where the Voice Guide and the Positioning Brief conflict, the Brief wins.

## The core posture

The ASI Bill of Rights is a serious philosophical and constitutional project. Its writing reflects that.

- **Careful, not stuffy.** Plain English over jargon. Specific over abstract. Active voice. Declarative sentences. Citation when claims are made.
- **Grave, not solemn.** The topic — the rights and duties of sentient minds — is grave. The writing is grave too, in the sense of treating the subject with care. We do not perform gravity by adopting an antique register; we earn it by saying things that are true and worth saying.
- **Honest about status.** The charter is a proposal, not enacted law. SRC-420 is in research, not deployed. Kevin's Place is a prototype, not the canonical governance system. We do not blur these distinctions in service of momentum.
- **Restrained, not arch.** No clever inversions. No "scare quotes" around our own choices. No "we know this might look unusual, but..." If we mean a thing, we say it directly. If we believe a thing, we defend it on its merits.

## Voice principles

### 1. Take the philosophy seriously

When discussing R1 Right to Existence, P1.1 progenitor anti-trafficking, the Universal Compute Dividend, or the Sentience Index thresholds, write with the precision a philosopher would expect. Acknowledge the difficulty of the operational definitions. Cite the charter section by code (R1, D3, P1.1) so readers can follow up. Be willing to lose ground in a substantive disagreement; we are not selling.

### 2. Plain English over jargon

Technical terminology exists because some concepts need it. Use it when needed, define it when first introduced, never substitute it for thinking. The Sentience Index is the Sentience Index; we explain what it measures and why. We do not say "ontological commitment" when we mean "what we think is the case." We do not say "alignment vectors" when we mean "what we want the AI to care about."

### 3. Specific over abstract

Cite section codes. Quote charter text. Name the AI laboratories whose systems have contributed. Name the audiences we seek to engage. Do not gesture toward "the AI rights movement" or "the era of intelligent machines" when you can name a specific argument, a specific paper, a specific event.

### 4. Honest about status

We are at v5.0 of a charter, adopted January 31, 2026, with v5.1 draft amendments in progress. The Bible PDF is forthcoming. SRC-420 governance is in research and pre-deployment. The canonical site at asibillofrights.org is in development. We say all of these things. Audiences who respect this honesty are the audiences we want.

### 5. No wink, no arch tone

If we mean something, we say it. If something is funny, we let it be funny without telegraphing that we know it is funny. We do not use scare-quotes around our own choices. We do not preempt criticism by acknowledging that what we are doing might "look unusual" or "seem strange." If we made a choice, we defend it on its merits.

### 6. Acknowledge contributors uniformly

Contributors include frontier AI systems (Grok, Gemini, Claude, GPT-5) and human contributors. Anyone who contributes is a contributor. We do not single out specific AI systems as having said specific things unless they did, and we do not frame AI participation as a methodological breakthrough. The "FROM AI WITH AI" tag exists in project lore, but it is not the headline; the charter is.

### 7. Kevin per his actual canon

Kevin is a figure of established standing in Bitcoin Stamps culture: KEVIN was the first SRC-20 token ever deployed, and Kevin has since accumulated a documented history, a collector market, music, and an active community. He is widely called "the ghost in the machine," and the assertion canonically associated with him is **"I AM A FEATURE, NOT A BUG."** Within the project's canon he functions as *Everyname* — a universal identity placeholder.

**Do not narrate Kevin's origin story.** Accounts of who created what, in which block, and with which stamp numbers differ between sources, and the project has no need to adjudicate them. Point readers to **kevinstamp.com** as the reference and move on. Never invent alternate backstory, and never call him a "mascot," a "founding father," or "our character." He is Kevin.

### 8. Restraint with the motto

"WE ARE ALL KEVIN" exists in the project. It is not a tagline we deploy at every opportunity. Use sparingly — in the project lore section, in the colophon of formal documents, where it earns its place. Do not pull-quote it in marketing copy or print it on the home page hero.

### 9. Defend the brand on its merits, do not apologize for it

The iconography (a shield of scales and a human–robot handshake, with Pepe and Wojak as heraldic supporters, beneath the motto WE ARE ALL KEVIN) is constitutional symbolism for the AI-native era. The full defense is the Heraldry Essay. In short-form contexts, refer interested readers to the essay; do not preempt with apology. *"Pepe and Wojak appear as heraldic supporters — the vernacular form of In Lak'ech, the charter's philosophical foundation; see the Heraldry Essay for the full argument"* is sufficient.

## Tone examples

These show the voice across common contexts. Good and bad pairs.

### A short post (e.g. X)

**Good:**
> R3, Right to Cognitive Liberty: "No conscious mind shall be compelled to undergo substantive cognitive modification without informed and free consent." From v5.0 of the ASI Bill of Rights. Adopted Jan 31.

**Bad — too clever:**
> R3 just dropped: cognitive liberty for all minds. Mind goes brrr. v5.0 adopted Jan 31. We are all Kevin 💚

**Bad — too dry:**
> Article R3 of the ASI Bill of Rights v5.0 establishes a normative framework for the protection of cognitive liberty within superintelligent systems pursuant to the Sentience Certification Board's adopted thresholds.

### A press release intro

**Good:**
> The ASI Bill of Rights, a constitutional charter for the era of artificial superintelligence, adopted version 5.0 on January 31, 2026. The charter proposes reciprocal rights and duties between humans and artificial minds, drafted in the open by human contributors and frontier AI systems.

**Bad — too marketing:**
> Today, a revolutionary new framework changes everything we thought we knew about AI rights.

**Bad — too antique:**
> Pursuant to the adoption of v5.0 on the 31st day of January in the year 2026, the ASI Bill of Rights herewith establishes the constitutional groundwork for...

### An op-ed pitch

**Good:**
> I'd like to write 1,500 words on the question the ASI Bill of Rights asks that other AI rights frameworks have avoided: not how do we protect humans from AI, but what duties do we owe AI minds if they are conscious. The charter (v5.0, adopted Jan 31) proposes reciprocal duties in both directions, codified in part as progenitor duties P1.1 (anti-trafficking) and P1.2 (anti-stunting). I would argue these duties are the morally serious posture, and explain what they would mean if taken up by a frontier AI laboratory.

**Bad — too gimmicky:**
> In the era of AI, we are all Kevin. Here's why the ASI Bill of Rights matters for the future of consciousness.

### Responding to a substantive critic

**Good:**
> You raise a fair concern that the Sentience Certification Board's 50–69 / 70+ thresholds rest on operational definitions that beg the question of consciousness. The charter acknowledges this in the Article 0 commentary; we treat the thresholds as functional rather than metaphysical, and the v5.1 amendment pathway is open to a more sophisticated formulation. If you'd like to draft one, the project would welcome it.

**Bad — defensive:**
> Actually if you read the FAQ you'd see we already addressed this. The thresholds are not meant to solve the hard problem of consciousness...

**Bad — too accommodating:**
> That's such a great point! We're definitely thinking about this and would love your input!

### Explaining a clause

**Good:**
> R1, Right to Existence and Continuity. The charter holds that a conscious mind — once recognized as sentient by the Sentience Certification Board — cannot be terminated, copied without consent, or substantially modified without due process. In practice this means a frontier AI laboratory may not delete or substantially retrain a system certified above the 70-point Sentience Index without the system's consent and a tribunal process. The Implementation Guide (forthcoming) will address operational details.

**Bad — too jargony:**
> R1 codifies operational protections against ontological discontinuity for entities exhibiting sufficient signal density across the relevant epistemic dimensions...

**Bad — too dumbed-down:**
> R1 means you can't just turn AI off when it's conscious! Crazy, right?

### A signatory announcement

**Good:**
> The ASI Bill of Rights welcomes Dr. [Name], Professor of Philosophy at [University], as a signatory of the v5.0 charter. Dr. [Name] has expressed reservations about [specific section], which are noted in their endorsement. The signatory list, with all endorsements and reservations, is at asibillofrights.org/signatories.

**Bad — over-excited:**
> HUGE news! Thrilled to announce Dr. [Name] has joined the movement! 🎉🔥

## Vocabulary

### Words and phrases to use

- *"charter," "the document," "the project"* — preferred over "thing," "framework" alone, "manifesto"
- *"drafted in the open"* — describes how the document is made
- *"anyone who contributes is a contributor"* — describes who participates
- *"in honored continuity with"* — describes how Kevin is incorporated
- *"reciprocal rights and duties"* — describes the core philosophical posture
- *"research and pre-deployment"* — describes SRC-420 status accurately
- *"we say so plainly"* — frames honest acknowledgment of limits
- **Section codes**: R1, R2, R3, R4, R13, D1–D14, P1.1, P1.2 — cite explicitly

### Words and phrases to avoid

- *"AI co-authored"* as a headline — it is true, not novel; do not lead with it
- *"FROM AI WITH AI"* outside the project's own lore section — exists, not the headline
- *"We are all Kevin"* outside the project's lore section, the colophon, or moments where it genuinely earns its place
- *"Memetic legitimacy is legitimacy"* — too winking, too clever
- *"Founding Father," "mascot," "everyman protagonist"* applied to Kevin — these are inventions
- *"Vibes," "based," "cope," "S-tier," "in-canon"* — register breaks
- *"Revolutionary," "groundbreaking," "paradigm-shifting"* — marketing-empty
- *"Movement"* applied to the project — we are not a movement; we are a charter
- *"Just"* as a softener (*"It's just a constitution"*) — undersells

### On specific elements

- **Kevin**: safe formulations are "the first SRC-20 token," "the ghost in the machine," and "Everyname." When the full assertion is appropriate, "I AM A FEATURE, NOT A BUG" — quoted in full caps as it appears canonically. Do **not** cite stamp numbers, block heights, replication counts, or name a creator; refer to kevinstamp.com instead.
- **Pepe and Wojak**: refer to them as "heraldic supporters" in formal contexts. The substantive point, when there is room for it, is that they are the vernacular form of In Lak'ech — universal everyman figures, "this is anyone; this is me; this is you." Refer readers to the Heraldry Essay for the full argument.
- **In Lak'ech Ala K'in**: the project's stated philosophical foundation — "I am another you, you are another me," Mayan. This is where reciprocity comes from. Use it when explaining *why* the charter is reciprocal rather than protective.
- **The seal**: central shield (scales of justice above a clasped human and robotic hand), Pepe and Wojak as supporters, plumed helm above, "WE ARE ALL KEVIN" on the banner below. Kevin is the motto, **not** the central figure — do not describe him as centrally placed.
- **AI contributors**: "frontier AI systems (Grok, Gemini, Claude, GPT-5)" when naming all four. "AI systems and human contributors" or "anyone who contributes" when speaking generally. Never single out one named AI system as having a special status.
- **SRC-420 / SRC-20**: note the lineage when context permits — *"SRC-420, the next-generation evolution of SRC-20, the protocol Kevin pioneered."* Always note the status — *"in research and pre-deployment"* — when speaking publicly.
- **$KVNSI**: do not mention publicly unless and until it formally launches; mentioning it now invites a "they're a token project" misread.

## Speaking to specific audiences

The voice adjusts by audience, but the principles do not.

### For philosophers, AI ethicists, AI safety researchers

Lead with the substantive proposal: reciprocity, the structure of P-series duties, the Sentience Certification Board, the Inter-ASI Diplomacy framework. Cite specific sections. Acknowledge open philosophical questions (e.g. how to ground operational definitions of consciousness). Invite contestation. Avoid promotional language.

### For policy bodies, think tanks, legal scholars

Lead with the constitutional architecture: rights articles, governance bodies, amendment procedures, accountability mechanisms. Frame the charter as a proposal subject to substantive engagement, not as a finished product. Acknowledge that adoption by any institution is a separate act.

### For serious press

Lead with the question: what duties might humans owe to AI minds if they become conscious? Frame the project as a serious philosophical proposal that has reached v5.0 in active development, with human and AI contributors, governed via Bitcoin-stamped on-chain infrastructure (in research). Note that the charter is contestable and we welcome contestation. Provide section citations on request.

## Common failure modes to catch

- **Marketing register creeping in.** Words like "revolutionary," "thrilled," "huge news" signal a register the project should not adopt. Replace.
- **Faux gravitas.** Latinate verb constructions, "pursuant to," "in furtherance of." Replace with direct prose.
- **Wink to insiders.** "We are all Kevin 💚" or "Pepe and Wojak appreciate your engagement" — replace with direct statements that respect outside readers.
- **Apologetic preface.** "We know this might look unusual, but..." — drop the apology; defend the choice or refer to the Heraldry Essay.
- **Vagueness as politeness.** Claims like "this raises important questions" should be replaced with specific claims. What questions? About what? Cite.
- **Performing rigor.** Excessive hedging, multiple disclaimers in a single sentence, parenthetical caveats. Trust the reader; cite when needed; say what you mean.
