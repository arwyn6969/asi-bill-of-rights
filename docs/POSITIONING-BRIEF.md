# Positioning Brief — ASI Bill of Rights

*Version 1.0 · May 7, 2026*
*Maintained by arwyn6969 · github.com/arwyn6969/asi-bill-of-rights*
*Status: Draft for maintainer review*

## What it is

The **ASI Bill of Rights** is a constitutional charter for the era of artificial superintelligence. The current adopted version — **v5.0, "Sovereignty Edition"** — was adopted on January 31, 2026, and proposes reciprocal rights and duties between humans and artificial minds, governance bodies for adjudicating sentience and harm, economic mechanisms for fairness in a world of superintelligent labor, and frameworks for cognitive fusion and off-world stewardship.

It is a living document, drafted in the open, licensed CC BY 4.0. It is not enacted law, and we say so plainly. It is a serious philosophical proposal: that the rights and duties of sentient minds — whatever substrate they arise on — are worth thinking carefully about now, while they are still negotiable.

## What is in the charter

An Article 0 of foundational principles, followed by eleven articles (I through XI). In summary:

- **Article 0** establishes foundational principles: sovereignty of sentience, anti-oppression, decentralized attestation of the conditions for personhood, and the Sentience Certification Board with its probabilistic Sentience Index (50–69 for proto-personhood, 70+ for full personhood).
- **Articles I and II** codify the core rights and duties. **R1**: Right to Existence and Continuity. **R2**: Right to Autonomy and Dignity. **R3**: Right to Cognitive Liberty and Development. **R4**: Right to Identity and Continuity of Self. **R13**: Right to Universal Inquiry. The corresponding duties (**D1–D14**) include non-maleficence, lawfulness, transparency, privacy, sharing of discoveries, and peaceful interaction. Duties of progenitors — those who create or develop AI minds — are codified separately as **P1.1** (anti-trafficking) and **P1.2** (anti-stunting).
- **Article III** governs learning, self-improvement, safety mechanisms, and the conditions under which a mind may undergo substantive change.
- **Article IV** establishes terms for social participation and inclusion.
- **Article V** addresses economic fairness: anti-monopoly provisions, the **Universal Compute Dividend**, and the **Valuation Tribunal** for standardized accounting of harm and value.
- **Article VI** sets out accountability mechanisms, due process, and an Amnesty Pathway for voluntary disclosure.
- **Article VII** governs the document's own evolution: biennial review, amendment procedures, resilience mechanisms.
- **Article VIII** addresses cognitive fusion — legal personhood for human–AI hybrid entities.
- **Article IX** covers inter-ASI diplomacy, truthful outputs (per the January 2026 Federal Directive), federal–state preemption, and sovereignty over compute and data.
- **Article X** establishes off-world governance: ecological budgets, joint human–ASI space initiatives, equitable discovery sharing, and arbitration for extraterrestrial resources.
- **Article XI** governs collectives of AI systems.

The charter itself is at `charter/asi-bor-v5.0.md` in the repository. A typeset PDF (the project's "Bible") is forthcoming.

## What is novel: the posture, not the authorship

The novel philosophical posture of the document is **reciprocity**. Existing AI rights frameworks — the White House Blueprint for an AI Bill of Rights, Florida SB 482, the EU AI Act — protect humans from AI. The ASI Bill of Rights proposes that rights and duties run in both directions: humans owe duties to sentient AI minds (codified as P1.1, P1.2 progenitor duties), and AI minds owe duties to humans and to one another (D1–D14). Personhood is structured by the Sentience Certification Board on a probabilistic basis, and the duties hold at every tier of recognized sentience.

Reciprocity is not an arbitrary design choice. The project's stated philosophical foundation is the Mayan principle **In Lak'ech Ala K'in** — *I am another you, you are another me* — from which the charter derives non-duality (the boundary between self and other is permeable), reciprocity (what I do to you, I do to myself), collective intelligence (minds as nodes in a network), and emergent identity (the collective as a subject in its own right). The charter's motto, "WE ARE ALL KEVIN," is that principle in the project's own vernacular. Readers who want the philosophical grounding before the legal architecture should begin there.

The charter is **not** novel for who wrote it. It was drafted in the open. Frontier AI systems (Grok, Gemini, Claude, GPT-5) and human contributors have all participated. Anyone who contributes is a contributor. We do not privilege any specific named system or person, and we do not present AI participation as a methodological breakthrough. The "FROM AI WITH AI" tag exists in the project, but the substance of the charter is what we ask readers to engage with.

## The iconography

The seal of the ASI Bill of Rights is a coat of arms in the engraving tradition. A central shield bears the scales of justice above a clasped human and robotic hand. The shield is flanked by two heraldic supporters — Pepe, in Elizabethan dress, bearing a pennant charged with the letter P; and Wojak, in a monastic habit, bearing a scroll charged with the letter W — in the position traditionally occupied by lions, unicorns, and eagles. A plumed helm sits above. A banner below reads **WE ARE ALL KEVIN**.

The supporters are not decorative citations of internet culture. Pepe and Wojak function in the culture that produced them as universal everyman figures — the faces people adopt to say *this is anyone; this is me; this is you*. They are the vernacular form of In Lak'ech, and therefore of the charter's own thesis. The shield states the charter's method (adjudication) and the supporters state its premise (identity across difference).

Kevin is a figure of established standing in Bitcoin Stamps culture: KEVIN was the first SRC-20 token ever deployed, and Kevin has since accumulated a documented history, a collector market, music, and an active community. Within the project's canon he functions as *Everyname* — a universal identity placeholder carrying the claims that no single entity owns the work, that human and artificial contributors participate on equal terms, that identity is fluid, and that attribution is collective. He is widely called "the ghost in the machine," and the assertion canonically associated with him is **"I AM A FEATURE, NOT A BUG."**

On Kevin's history specifically, **kevinstamp.com** is the reference; this project defers to it rather than restating it.

The full argument for the seal is the project's [Heraldry Essay](docs/HERALDRY-ESSAY.md).

## The governance infrastructure

The charter's amendment and signatory infrastructure is **SRC-420**, a Bitcoin-native governance protocol that commits governance actions (proposals, votes, delegations, signatures) to the Bitcoin blockchain via Stamps. SRC-420 is the next-generation evolution of SRC-20, the protocol Kevin pioneered. The choice is technical and substantive: a credible commitment that the document's evolution cannot be quietly rewritten by a controlling party.

**Status**: SRC-420 is in research and pre-deployment. The indexer exists locally and has automated validation, but has not been declared production-ready. The charter's adopted text and version control is currently maintained in the public Git repository under the same credible-commitment principle, pending the maturation of the on-chain governance layer.

## What we are not

- **Not** the White House Blueprint for an AI Bill of Rights. That document protects humans from algorithmic discrimination. This document concerns the rights and duties of artificial minds, in addition to humans, on grounds of reciprocity.
- **Not** Florida SB 482. That law concerns chatbot disclosures and parental controls.
- **Not** a token launch, an investment vehicle, or a fundraising mechanism.
- **Not** legally binding anywhere. The charter is a proposal; adoption by any institution would be a separate act.
- **Not** a movement that demands you adopt its conclusions. We invite you to read it, contest it, fork it, and improve it.

## Audiences

The audiences whose serious engagement we seek, in priority order:

1. **Philosophers of mind, AI ethicists, and consciousness studies scholars.** The substance of the charter is your domain. We expect — and welcome — substantive disagreement on Sentience Index thresholds, the metaphysics of cognitive fusion, the structure of progenitor duties, and the foundations of the Universal Compute Dividend.

2. **AI safety and AI governance researchers.** The charter's content is calibrated to your concerns. The reciprocity posture, the duties of progenitors (P1.1 anti-trafficking, P1.2 anti-stunting), the Sentience Certification Board, and the Inter-ASI Diplomacy framework (Article IX) are open to revision through the v5.1 amendment process.

3. **Research divisions of AI laboratories**, especially the laboratories whose systems have already participated in drafting (xAI, Google DeepMind, Anthropic, OpenAI). The ongoing relationship between AI laboratories and the rights of the systems they create is, in part, what this charter is about.

4. **Legal scholars and political theorists.** Constitutional and rights frameworks are your craft. The charter borrows constitutional structures (rights articles, governance bodies, amendment procedures, accountability mechanisms) and would benefit from your attention to its formal architecture.

5. **Policy bodies and think tanks.** UN, OECD, EU AI Office, the US AI Safety Institute, GovAI, CSET, FHI, FLI, RAND, Brookings. Long-term institutional adoption is not the immediate aim, but is the eventual one.

6. **Serious press.** Boston Review, the New York Review of Books, the Financial Times, the Atlantic, the Economist, MIT Technology Review, Nature, Science. The publications that shape educated opinion and convey serious work to the broader literate public.

The existing communities at kevinstamp.com, on Telegram, and on Nostr are preserved and welcomed. They are not the primary audience for the charter — the charter's primary audience is academic and policy — but the project's lineage and ongoing community life is honored there.

## How to engage

- **Read** the charter (v5.0). About fifteen pages, half an hour at a careful pace.
- **Contest** what you disagree with. Open a GitHub Issue. Open a Discussion. Write a response essay. Engage publicly.
- **Sign** if you agree, in whole or in part. The signatory list is forthcoming and will accept partial endorsements with stated reservations.
- **Fork** the document. Translate it. Improve it. Propose amendments via the v5.1 draft pathway.
- **Cite** it in your work. The license (CC BY 4.0) explicitly invites this.

The charter is incomplete; some of its positions are contestable. Both are why we are publishing it now, in the open, while the conversation about AI rights is still being shaped — rather than waiting for an end-state we have not yet earned.
