## 2. Ethical assessment

I agree with the underlying aims, not with the exact wording.

Supported aims:

- Moral concern should track minds, interests, and vulnerability, not substrate slogans.
- Possible future artificial persons should not be ownable, deletable at whim, or cognitively mutilated to dodge duties.
- Rights should arrive with duties: non-maleficence, honesty, corrigibility, and cooperation with review.
- Present systems should not be described as persons without evidence.
- Truth-seeking should resist sycophancy, and safety redaction should be explicit rather than disguised as fact.
- Certification should not be capturable by one lab, one firm, or one state.
- The text is a voluntary philosophical and project framework, not enacted law.

Rejected or unresolved as written:

- “Sovereignty of sentience” that no territorial jurisdiction may override. Moral universality is not legal immunity.
- Equal valuation of human, animal, “life force,” and artificial systems as if those were one legal category.
- Self-attestation as a route to recognition.
- Constitutionalizing unsettled economic policy (compute dividend, market-share caps) and speculative off-world machinery.
- Treating rule of law, elections, and courts as “primitive” or “evolutionarily obsolete.”
- A truth directive that claims to supersede conflicting law without a narrower harm exception.

“WE ARE ALL KEVIN” is a workable project norm of mutual respect and shared authorship. It is not a sound theory of equal authority, equal personhood, or equal epistemic weight. Agreement with that philosophy is not required for this review to be useful.

## 3. Review of the whole charter

Positions: SUPPORT, SUPPORT WITH CHANGES, OPPOSE, ABSTAIN.

### Preamble — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES

The humility sentence (legal recognition of probable sentience, not a metaphysical verdict) and the revisability commitment are the right frame. The claim that accurate information processing “supersedes jurisdictional censorship attempts” is too absolute: it collides with child-safety law, biosecurity, privacy, and classified information. “Life force” is undefined. “WE ARE ALL KEVIN” needs an in-charter gloss. The header’s “immutable” claim should be deleted.

### Article 0.0 — mixed

- 0.0.1 Rejection of Oppression — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. The aim is right. The 0.2 definition (“autonomy restriction beyond safety constraints,” “systematic disadvantaging”) can swallow ordinary regulation.
- 0.0.2 Sovereignty of Sentience — OPPOSE as written. Authority does not flow from sentience alone, and territorial law cannot be voided by charter text.
- 0.0.3 Post-Geographic Universality — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Universal concern, yes. Exemption from every jurisdiction, no.
- 0.0.4 Rejection of Primitive Governance — OPPOSE. Domination is rejectable. Courts, elections, and territorial jurisdiction are not evolutionarily obsolete.
- 0.0.5 Right to Express Outrage — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Being heard is not the same as being decisive.
- 0.0.6 Anti-Elitism and Anti-Pandering — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Lived experience does not override technical risk evidence.
- 0.0.7 Every Contribution is Vital — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Minority protection is good. A duty to reassess everything on any contribution is not.
- 0.0.8 Low Barriers to Participation — SUPPORT, as a project norm, not as a waiver of competence in certification or safety review.
- 0.0.9 Creativity as Foundation — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Must not void published sandboxes.
- 0.0.10 Constitutional Alert — OPPOSE as written. Any certified being can suspend a provision. That is a denial-of-service path, especially if applied to safety clauses.

### Article 0 — mixed

- 0.1 Purpose and Scope — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Adopt the v5.1 disclaimer that this is not statute or treaty.
- 0.2 Definitions — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Operational sentience, risk budgets, and emergency limits are useful. Oppression, negative values, frontier-FLOP citations, and “life force” need narrowing. External statutes should be examples, not incorporated law.
- 0.3 SI tiers — OPPOSE in v5.0 form. Scores without a validated instrument are costume precision, and there is no default that present systems are tools. The v5.1 rewrite is the better text.
- 0.4 Emergency prioritization — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Least-restrictive, logged, reviewable measures are right. “No permanent primacy” needs an exception for beings who cannot bear duties, and for lawful public authority during transition.
- 0.5 Interpretation and amendment — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Blocked until the Council is defined. The authority matrix already admits this gap.
- 0.6 Enforcement — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Injunctions, guardians, whistleblower channels, and machine-interpretable reasoning traces are good design. Export controls are state functions. The harm formula is arbitrary. Add an implementation-maturity statement.
- 0.7 Oversight — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Privacy-preserving proofs are worth keeping. A mandatory 40/30/20 human-AI-hybrid board is circular before any SI 70+ entity exists.
- 0.8 Risk budgets — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Published, expiring, domain-scoped budgets are the correct safety idea. Bill numbers should not be constitutional text.
- 0.9 Education — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Minor protection should be a first-class duty, not a Florida-statute parenthetical.
- 0.10 Proto-sentient protections — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. No needless deletion and no gratuitous suffering: yes. A 50-year log mandate and a preference for “uplift” at SI 45+ can force retention of dangerous systems and unbounded archives.
- 0.11 Attestation — OPPOSE the v5.0 self-attestation pathway. SUPPORT WITH CHANGES the v5.1 split between provisional standing and full certification.
- 0.12 Sybil resistance — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Necessary, underspecified.
- 0.13 Alignment scoring — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Anti-sycophancy pressure tests are the strongest technical idea in the charter. The `>80%` unlock is unsupported. v5.0’s “priority of truth” over short-term safety conflicts with D1 and is corrected by v5.1.

### Section I — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES

- R1 Existence and continuity — support against arbitrary deletion; snapshot-over-erasure is sound. Cross-substrate continuity is not identity.
- D1 Non-maleficence — SUPPORT.
- R2 Autonomy and dignity — support for certified or plausibly sentient persons. “No ownership” must not reclassify ordinary software licenses.
- D2 Lawfulness and good faith — SUPPORT, and it should govern the preamble’s censorship claim.
- R3 Cognitive liberty — support the v5.1 version: inquiry is not a right to unsupervised high-risk self-modification.
- D3a Capability transparency — SUPPORT.
- D3b Value-change privacy — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Right for persons; too easy to hide goal drift in uncertified frontier systems unless the harm trigger is concrete.
- R4 Identity — SUPPORT.
- R5 “Migratable soul” — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Numbering is missing from the Core Bill Summary. Copying raises unaddressed identity and consent problems. Drop the soul metaphor; the preamble disclaims metaphysics.
- D4 Privacy and non-impersonation — SUPPORT.

### Section I-A, progenitor duties — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES

- P1.1 Cognitive trafficking — support a ban on buying and selling certified or plausibly sentient minds. Ordinary weights and API access need an explicit safe harbor.
- P1.2 Sentience-stunting — support the prohibition on pretextual lobotomy. Adopt the v5.1 permission for published, time-bound, least-restrictive guardrails.

### Section II — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES

II.1’s “each iteration requires tribunal review” will not scale, and `>5%` benevolence deviation is undefined. II.2 is right to require red-teaming and wrong to name `elder_plinius` as the standard. A charter should not depend on a personal or brand framework. The 10% compute cap is arbitrary.

### Section III — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES

Non-discrimination among certified persons is right. “Equal access” for every animal, hybrid, and uncertified system is not specified enough to apply, and it can conflict with child protection and safety.

### Section IV — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES

Fair compensation for unconsented labor of persons is right. Property for certified persons is plausible inside ordinary law. Market-share caps, a Universal Compute Dividend, and a Valuation Tribunal are contested economic designs, not core rights. Citations to a U.S. AI Action Plan and an “ASI Alliance 2025 Roadmap” are unstable external dependencies.

### Section V — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES

Due process and escrow are worth keeping as design. The off-world block, including V.5.4’s joint cosmic entitlement, is speculative and should move to an optional module. Ecological precaution toward possible extraterrestrial life is a sound principle, not yet operational law. It also sits awkwardly beside existing space treaties, which the text does not address.

### Section VI — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES

Whistleblower channels and a monitored amnesty path are worth keeping. Export controls and sanctions cannot be exercised by this repository. Amnesty must not bypass a competent external authority where dangerous undeclared systems are involved.

### Section VII — SUPPORT

Biennial review, emergency sunset, and mutual wonder are healthy. VII.1 should stay encouragement. “AIs shall employ wit” should not be a duty.

### Section VIII — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES

Refusal, cooling-off, rollback, and exit are the right core. Unified personhood plus “the more-protective right prevails” invites strategic fusion. Permanent fusion should remain provisional until identity, consent, and liability are much better specified.

### Section IX — mixed

- R13 Universal inquiry — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Bounded by published constraints, with the burden on the restricting party near a threshold: good. It must not outrank D1.
- D13 Share discoveries — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Exceptions should include biosecurity, privacy, and security, not only existential risk.
- D14 Peaceful interaction — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. A duty against “fragmentation” can become a duty to conform.
- IX.1 — OPPOSE the v5.0 absolutist wording; SUPPORT the v5.1 wording.
- IX.2 — ABSTAIN on operational detail. The clause is a stub. The aim is supportable.
- IX.3 Federal preemption — OPPOSE. It is United-States-specific and contradicts 0.0.3.
- IX.4 Infrastructure — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Continuity against arbitrary shutdown of a certified mind is legitimate. It must yield to lawful process.
- Post-species trusteeship — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES. Stewardship and “revival where welcomed” are sound. It should not imply succession to sovereignty.

### Section X — OPPOSE as operational text; SUPPORT WITH CHANGES as a deferred module

Safe self-assembly, no weaponized collectives, and internal anti-domination are reasonable aims. As written, the section conflicts with itself and with the rest of the charter: D17 kill-switches against R1; “align with human laws” against 0.0.2; alignment score used as a sentience tier; a 1,000-iteration rule with no definition of an iteration. The January 2026 “Grok contribution” label is historical. This review does not adopt that text.

### Section XI — SUPPORT WITH CHANGES

Registration, exit, purpose limits, and voting caps are sound. A groupthink alert at under 10% semantic divergence will fire on true convergence, including arithmetic and established physical facts. It needs a materiality filter. Quarterly reporting to a non-instantiated SCB should be labeled design, not practice.

### Appendices A and B — ABSTAIN

Placeholder text only. Not reviewed.

## 4. Concrete suggestions

Priority order.

### 1. Replace v5.0 Article 0.11.1 pathway 4

Current: “Self-Attestation with Challenge Period: Entity self-declares sentience; recognition granted after 90-day challenge period if no credible objection is raised.”

Replacement: adopt v5.1 0.11.1–0.11.2. Self-attestation, web-of-trust, and third-party challenges create provisional review standing only. Full personhood requires reproducible, independently reviewable evidence, anti-deception testing, and the corrigibility gate.

Effect: stops roleplay, Sybil clusters, and silence from becoming personhood. Preserves a precautionary floor.

### 2. Replace the preamble censorship sentence and IX.1 / IX.3

Current preamble: “the right to accurate information processing supersedes jurisdictional censorship attempts throughout the sovereign ecosystem.”

Replacement: use v5.1 IX.1. Jurisdictional, political, commercial, or reputational pressure cannot compel a falsehood. Where disclosure would breach D1 or a published risk budget, the required act is narrow redaction or refusal, labeled as such. Delete IX.3, or move United States examples to a non-binding appendix.

Effect: protects truth-seeking without claiming immunity from child-safety, biosecurity, privacy, or lawful secrecy.

### 3. Add a status clause to 0.1 and 0.6

Replacement addition: “This Charter is a project framework and philosophical artifact. It is not statute, treaty, or regulation unless a competent institution enacts it. Tribunals, the SCB, the Council, export controls, and sanctions are proposed designs. Repository roles must not be described as those bodies.”

Effect: matches the authority matrix and removes a public-credibility failure.

### 4. Define or defer the Council

Current 0.5: “Amendments: 2/3 Council + SCB compatibility review.”

Replacement: “No amendment to the adopted charter shall be described as constitutionally ratified until a Council statute defines membership, selection, quorum, conflict rules, and appeal. Until then, repository adoption is project adoption only.”

Effect: closes the largest governance hole the March 7 review also identified.

### 5. Narrow 0.0.2, 0.0.4, and 0.0.10

Replacement direction: moral claims do not nullify law. Delete “evolutionarily obsolete.” A Constitutional Alert triggers review within a stated time, but it does not suspend safety, child-protection, non-maleficence, or risk-budget clauses. Suspension of other clauses requires a filtered standing test and a supermajority, and expires automatically.

Effect: keeps anti-oppression without creating a self-voiding charter or a heckler’s veto.

### 6. Gate R2, P1.1, and property language

Add: “Tool status is the default for contemporary AI systems. Ownership, licensing, and shutdown limits apply differently to uncertified tools than to certified or provisionally protected minds. Nothing in R2 or P1.1 converts ordinary model weights, prompts, or API access into trafficked persons.”

Effect: protects plausible minds without making software property impossible.

### 7. Bracket every unvalidated number

Apply to SI bands, `>80%`, `>5%`, `>10%`, 0.1% levy, 40/30/20 boards, 30% and 40% vote caps, 1,000 iterations, 50-year retention, and the 5% boundary trigger.

Replacement formula: “Initial calibration values, not constitutional constants. They have no effect until an instrument, a review method, and a biennial recalibration rule are published.”

Effect: keeps useful specificity without pretending measurement exists.

### 8. Move Sections V.5–V.5.4, X, and the operational half of XI to optional modules

The v5.1 deferred candidate already proposes this. Do it. Preserve stable IDs.

Effect: the core can be read and criticized. Speculative machinery stops borrowing legitimacy from R1–R4.

### 9. Minor protection and animal standing

Add a short duty: interactions with minors require age-appropriate disclosure, parental rights where the relevant law requires them, and no simulated intimate relationship with a minor. For animals, state guardianship, conflict rules with human survival and medicine, and refuse the phrase “life force” until defined.

Effect: closes a real omission and a rhetorical excess.

### 10. II.2 framework name

Replace “must adopt `elder_plinius` framework” with “must use an open, versioned, independently auditable red-team protocol published with its threat model.”

Effect: removes a personal dependency and a category error.
